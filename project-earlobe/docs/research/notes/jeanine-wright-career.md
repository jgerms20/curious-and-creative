# Jeanine Wright: Professional Background and Career

*Research notes for Project Earlobe (Curious & Creative Network). Compiled 2026-10-11. Covers her public professional record only. No personal contact details, home information or family details were collected, and none should be added.*

## TL;DR

Jeanine Wright (she also uses her full professional name, Jeanine Percival Wright) is a Los Angeles media and tech executive who started as an entertainment and IP lawyer. She was the first General Counsel at Media Temple (sold to GoDaddy in 2013), General Counsel at ZestFinance, co-founder and first investor in Simplecast (sold to SiriusXM in 2020), CFO at SiriusXM's ad-tech unit AdsWizz, and then COO and General Manager of Wondery from about February 2022 to 2024. That was after Amazon bought Wondery, so she ran operations under Amazon. She did not do the acquisition deal. In 2023 she co-founded Inception Point AI, whose Quiet Please network became the best-known "AI podcast factory": thousands of shows, about 3,000 episodes a week, about $1 per episode, and break-even at roughly 20 listens (company claims). She defended it loudly, calling critics "lazy luddites." In late September 2026 she pivoted. The consumer network shrank to about 300 unmonetized shows, and the company now sells B2B "content transformation" under the **Liquid Studios** brand. Inception Point AI remains the parent, and Podnews also saw the name "Liquify AI." She is a frequent podcast guest and conference panelist, and LinkedIn is her most visible public business channel. For a small AI-podcast studio with a journalism background, the strongest angle is trust. She stayed away from hard news, and critics hammered her shows' quality. Project Earlobe's automated Trust Stack, its local-news focus, and the Los Angeles test market are all things she has reason to care about now that her company sells to publishers.

## 3. Wondery: what she did

- **Role:** COO and General Manager, later called "Global COO." She ran sales and revenue, licensing, partnerships, operations, strategy and expansion into new markets.
- **Context:** At the time Wondery was Amazon Music's podcast studio, home of narrative hits such as *Dr. Death* and *Dirty John*, and it was moving toward a Wondery+ subscription model. These notes do not cover her specific achievements there, and the public coverage is thin.
- **Why it matters for us:** She has seen premium, human-made narrative podcasting at scale, with its talent and production costs, from the P&L side. That experience shapes her economic argument for AI.

## 4. Inception Point AI → Liquid Studios

### Founding and team
- **Founded:** 2023. Tracxn and AFP say 2023 and PitchBook says 2024. The Quiet Please download totals are counted from September 2023.
- **Co-founders listed on Crunchbase:** Jeanine Wright, Joshua (Josh) Taylor, Katie Brown and William Corbin. The old website listed five co-founders and executives. The new Liquid Studios site lists only Wright and **Josh Taylor (Co-Founder & Chief Production Officer)**, per Podnews and the About page.
- **Funding:** PitchBook reports a $5.05M seed round dated 2026-01-29. Tracxn records the same amount as a Series A on 2026-01-20. Activate Venture Partners lists Inception Point AI as an active portfolio company, and PitchBook also lists NVIDIA Inception. Latka separately claims about $770K in 2025 revenue and calls the company bootstrapped. Its data is low quality.
- **Team size:** 8 employees (AFP, TheWrap, 2025). Podnews counted "four content staff."

### The Quiet Please model (2025 to mid-2026)
- More than 5,000 shows and about 3,000 episodes a week (THR, Sept 2025). Later company figures went higher: more than 10,000 active shows (Bloomberg, via Futurism), more than 11,000 active shows and 300,000 episodes (Podcast Show session page and Podcast Advertising Playbook summary, May–June 2026), and 10,000 episodes a week (company figure quoted in a May 2026 blog).
- **Cost:** about $1 or less per episode depending on length and complexity. Each episode took about an hour to produce.
- **Break-even:** about 20 listens per episode, before overhead. This rests on programmatic ads at a high assumed CPM (around $50 according to one secondary source). She argued that automation lowers the ad-sales threshold from "several thousand downloads" to a few dozen (AFP).
- **Audience:** 10M downloads since Sept 2023 (THR). Other reports give 12M lifetime downloads and 400K subscribers, or "11M listeners" (Hume case study, vendor marketing).
- **Content:** hyper-local weather and pollen shows by city ("Weather in Boston/Austin/Milwaukee…"), biographies, edutainment and true-crime-style series. A co-founder said they did not do hard news. They released a Charlie Kirk show within about an hour of his shooting (Podnews), and critics cited it.
- **Hosts:** 50+ named AI personalities (e.g., "Claire/Clare Delish" for food, "Nigel Thistledown" for nature), described as an "all-AI talent agency."
- **Tech:** an "AI-orchestration layer" rather than a model maker. It pulls from several LLMs and data sources depending on the topic (Freakonomics). Secondary coverage cites about 184 agents and models, including the major commercial chatbots, for research, drafting and fact-checking. Voice came from **Hume AI's EVI** (Hume case study). No ElevenLabs link was found.
- **Disclosure:** AI is disclosed in every episode. Wright told AFP this causes "very little drop-off" in listeners. One secondary summary says audio disclosures run on shows longer than 3 minutes and written notes appear on all of them.
- **Criticism:** Indicator listened to 19 episodes and found glitches, groans and repeated words. Others called it "AI slop," SEO spam and audience-dilution. At the Podcast Show London (May 2026) she was asked about legacy shows giving bad health information and reportedly said they would not be removed.

### The pivot (Sept 2026)
- On Lead Balloon Ep. 73 (released about 2026-09-29) and in Podnews coverage the same day, Wright said the company had effectively been running two businesses. It chose the B2B side as having more potential and fitting what it promised investors.
- Quiet Please is down to **about 300 active shows**, which the company no longer tries to monetize. Synthetic hosts stay available as a client offering, and the consumer shows serve as a sandbox.
- She called some of the earlier publicity a "PR strategy" and said the backlash did not cause the pivot. She also told Podnews the catalog itself was never a stunt.
- Freakonomics (Ep. 690, 2026-10-09) noted that a few weeks after its interview the company quietly took down its public network and rebranded as Liquid Studios.
- **Liquid Studios offer:** a managed service that turns a media company's existing catalog into video, audio, short-form and "AI-readable" formats, then distributes and measures it. List price is **$10 per finished minute of video**, so a 60-minute episode costs about $600. Audio-only production is priced separately. Target buyers are media companies, large creators and publishers. Liquid Studios is not affiliated with Liquid AI, the LLM company.

## 5. Public philosophy, hot takes and economics

These are paraphrased, with short attributed fragments only.

- **On critics:** People who call all AI content slop are probably "lazy luddites" (The Hollywood Reporter, Sept 2025). She repeated the jab at the Podcast Show London in May 2026 and added that plenty of human-made content is slop too. The room did not take it well.
- **Strategy:** The near-term goal was to "play the volume game" (AFP, Oct 2025). AI fills a "content desert" of niche topics that human producers will never cover profitably.
- **Unit economics:** If an episode costs about $1, even a pollen podcast that 50 people hear is unit-profitable (THR). Traditional podcast companies struggle to find sustainable business models (Podnews Weekly Review, Sept 2025).
- **Identity:** The company is a tech company, not a media company or podcast network, and an orchestration layer rather than a model maker (Freakonomics, 2026).
- **Futurism:** She calls herself an "AI maximalist" (AI-Proof podcast listing). She is reported to have predicted that "half the people on the planet will be AI" in the near future (secondary reports). She has discussed whether AI-generated "people" should have rights, and has described fully autonomous AI hosts that choose their own episode topics and can be messaged in Slack (Podcast Show, 2026).
- **Disclosure and trust:** She favors labeling every episode and says audiences do not mind. She has described human review only for sensitive topics and says topics are chosen to avoid areas that are hard for models.
- **Post-pivot economics (inference from the Sept 2026 interviews):** Programmatic monetization of fully synthetic consumer feeds did not prove out as a business she wanted to keep. The value moved to B2B services: transforming publishers' existing IP at a per-minute price.
- **Earlier advocacy:** At Simplecast she ran a podcast monetization and pay-gap survey aimed at the under-payment of women and people of color in podcasting.

## 8. Mutual-interest angles for Curious & Creative

These are our own reasoning, labeled as inference.

1. **Trust is the gap she was criticized for.** The criticism of Quiet Please centered on accuracy, glitches, health misinformation and breaking-news speed (the Kirk episode). Project Earlobe's automated Trust Stack (source verifier, fact checker, bias checker before auto-publish) answers exactly that. Pitch it as "what we learned from the first wave of AI networks, and how we're building verification in," not as "you got it wrong." She reacts badly to critics.
2. **Hard news is the lane she avoided.** Her co-founder said they did not do hard news. Our journalism-school DNA and local daily briefs (Columbia SC, LA) sit where she chose not to go, so we complement her rather than compete.
3. **Hyper-local unit economics.** Her city weather and pollen shows are the nearest real-world data on per-city automated audio. Ask what listen thresholds, CPMs and sponsor categories actually worked before the pivot. This is the single most useful question for our ROI model.
4. **She now sells to publishers.** Liquid Studios turns publishers' archives into new formats. Our cold-case and History-of-Hollywood archive shows, and any local newsroom partners we sign, could be joint opportunities or referrals. We could also be a reference customer.
5. **The LA test market means in-person access.** She is LA-based, and our LA launch gives a natural reason for coffee or a studio visit.
6. **Legal and IP expertise.** She is a former entertainment and IP litigator and GC. Our court trackers, true-crime and cold-case shows raise defamation, right-of-publicity and voice-likeness questions. Asking for her view on guardrails respects her actual expertise.
7. **Her advisory track record.** She has served on 5+ boards and advised Wonder Media Network through a sale. A light-touch advisor or office-hours ask is plausible once there is rapport. Don't open with it.
8. **The voice stack.** She used Hume EVI and we use ElevenLabs. A vendor-comparison conversation is low-stakes and concrete.
9. **Shared bet, different thesis.** We agree AI-native audio is coming. We differ on volume versus trust and local relevance. A respectful contrast makes a good guest conversation for one of our shows or for an industry podcast.

**Suggested first touch (inference):** a LinkedIn note of 2–3 sentences. Mention her Lead Balloon or Freakonomics comments, say we're building verified AI local-news briefs starting in LA and Columbia SC, and ask for 20 minutes on what she learned about hyper-local economics and trust before the Liquid Studios pivot.
