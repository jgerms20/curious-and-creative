## The short version

Host Stephen Dubner answers a listener question that arrives 10 to 20 times a year: how do podcasts really make money? He assembles, in the show's own words, "a producer, an agent, an economist, a media executive, and an AI evangelist". Our reading of the result *(inference)*: podcasting is a medium-sized, badly measured, hit-driven business. Money pools at the top, in talent, franchises, multi-year deals and whole-network sales. It does not pool in long-tail volume. The AI-volume bet fits that picture badly, and a postscript says the AI guest's public network was taken down weeks after her interview.

## What it means for us

| What the episode shows | So what for Earlobe *(inference)* | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Ads are a thin base. Typical CPMs are modest and about 1% of shows profit. | Our local briefs cannot live on ads alone (facts sheet, "five things", point 1). Plan on direct sponsors, B2B and services alongside ads. | 1, 3, 4 |
| Money sits in talent, IP and multi-year deals. | We have no human star. Our IP-like assets are the trusted brand, a public trust record, original consistent personas and the archive shows (Cold Case Files, History of Hollywood). Build those on purpose. | 1 |
| Nobody has clean numbers; Page wants a clean-up. | Publish ours: monthly transparency report, audited download counts, public corrections log. In an opaque market, believable data is a selling point. | 1, 2 |
| The loudest AI-volume champion retreated to serving existing content. | Supports our B2B and Studio Services hedges, but do not over-read it: Liquid Studios has no named customers (F-IPA-09). Test with 2 or 3 real buyers first. | 3, 4 |
| "One model, or a handful, is extremely risky." | Hold a tested fallback voice vendor (F-TTS-12 ElevenLabs API; F-TTS-23 Google Chirp 3 HD at ~$0.03 a minute), keep LLM tiers swappable, and keep several revenue lines. | all |
| The human audio world is hostile to AI-only shows. | Spoken disclosure at the end of every episode and in metadata, original disclosed personas, no fake humans, no "luddite" PR. Partner with local newsrooms and public radio rather than compete. | 1 |
| Wright's rule at scale: change or remove, do not fight. | For our strict lanes (courts, crime) write a fast correct-or-remove rule into the corrections policy. Decide where we differ from "just remove it". | 1, 2 |
| Network sales happen (Vox to Lupa). | "Build the network first, sell later" has a real exit shape. But those deals buy human talent and IP; an automated catalog has no proven resale value. | 1 |
