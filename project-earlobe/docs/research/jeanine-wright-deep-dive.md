## 4. The reverse-engineered business plan

*Everything here is **inference** built from reported facts: a plan that fits the evidence, not a document we have seen. Prices as of Oct 2026 — verify before buying.*

### 4.1 Their playbook

| Rule | What they did | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Push cost toward zero | Chain many models and a TTS voice; about $1 per episode | F-IPA-04 |
| Volume over hits | Thousands of thin feeds. A few big-city weather and biography feeds likely carried the dead ones (power law) | F-IPA-07 |
| Demand from search, not editors | Google Trends, plain keyword titles, about 5 title variants per idea, avoid topics models handle badly | IP note section 5 |
| Programmatic ads, no sales team | Dynamic ads through Spreaker; contextual pitch such as pollen shows for antihistamine advertisers | F-IPA-08 |

### 4.2 Pipeline, step by step

| # | Step | Likely how it worked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sense and package | Google Trends, social, news, weather, air quality, police blotters. A watchlist of people with existing bio series, so a news spike triggers an episode (the Kirk series came within about an hour). An agent writes concept, keyword title, art, host persona |
| 2 | Filter and research | Drop tricky topics up front (a topic gate, not a claim check). Search-grounded model gathers facts; structured feeds fill templates |
| 3 | Script | Draft, rewrite and consistency pass, probably on different vendors |
| 4 | Voice and assemble | Hume EVI-class voice per persona, AI music, ad cue points, loudness |
| 5 | Publish | Spreaker API to RSS to Apple, Spotify, Amazon, iHeart; clips to YouTube and Instagram |
| 6 | Learn | Keep winning titles, let losers go dormant. This loop made the flood that platforms read as spam |

**What the pivot proves:** ad-only synthetic volume did not survive for them. **What it does not prove:** that AI audio fails, or that local formats fail. Weather, pollen and crime logs were easy to produce; distribution and money were the problem *(inference)*.

## 6. Copy, avoid, do differently

| Copy | How we do it |
|---|---|
| Orchestration across vendors | Several model tiers behind one pipeline. ElevenLabs primary with a tested fallback voice such as Google Chirp 3 HD (F-TTS-23) |
| Cost and downloads per episode as core metrics | Budget Controller caps from day one |
| Disclosure on every episode; correct-or-remove fast | End of audio plus metadata (Apple requires it, F-HOST-15); she claims little listener drop-off (F-AUD-05, low-med). Written correction SLA |
| A B2B line from the same engine | Archive and catalog-to-audio for local publishers. Anchors: $10 per video minute, about $1 per audio episode (F-IPA-09) |

| Avoid | Our rule |
|---|---|
| Feed flooding (325 in a day) | Few feeds per market, slow human-plausible cadence. Weather, air quality and pollen live inside the daily briefs |
| Keyword-chasing on tragedies | Deaths, violence, minors and named private people need a higher bar, a cooling-off delay or human sign-off |
| Invented expert personas, voice clones of real people | Original synthetic voices, labeled |
| Combative PR, volume as the story | Lead with accuracy and show our work |
| Bad legacy content left up; glitchy audio | Re-check evergreen and archive episodes; automated audio QA gate before publish |
| Ad-only plan, or planning on a $50 CPM | Sourced CPMs are $12 to $22 (F-ADS-04 to 06) |
