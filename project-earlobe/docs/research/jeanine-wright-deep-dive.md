## 4. The reverse-engineered business plan

*Everything in this section is **inference** built from the reported facts. It describes a plan that fits the evidence, not a document we have seen. Prices as of Oct 2026 — verify before buying.*

### 4.1 Their playbook

| Rule | What they did | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Push cost toward zero | Chain many models and a TTS voice; about $1 per episode | F-IPA-04 |
| Volume over hits | Thousands of thin feeds. A few big-city weather and biography feeds likely carried the dead ones (power law) | F-IPA-07 |
| Demand from search, not editors | Google Trends, plain keyword titles, about 5 title variants per idea, avoid topics models handle badly | IP note section 5 |
| Programmatic ads, no sales team | Dynamic ads through Spreaker; contextual pitch such as pollen shows for antihistamine advertisers | F-IPA-08 |
| Personas as brand | 50 to 120+ named hosts pitched as cross-platform creators | F-IPA-06 |
| Noise as marketing | Loud, combative publicity; she later called part of it a deliberate PR strategy | Lead Balloon 73 |

### 4.2 Pipeline, step by step

| # | Step | Likely how it worked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sense demand | Google Trends, social trends, news, weather, air quality and police blotters. A watchlist of people with existing bio series, so a news spike triggers an episode (the Kirk series came within about an hour) |
| 2 | Package a show | Agent writes concept, keyword title, art; assigns a host persona and voice |
| 3 | Risk filter | Drop tricky topics up front. A topic-level gate, not a claim check |
| 4 | Research | Search-grounded model; structured feeds fill templates directly |
| 5 | Script | Draft, rewrite and consistency pass, probably on different vendors |
| 6 | Voice | Hume EVI-class voice per persona, AI music |
| 7 | Assemble | Intro and outro, ad cue points, loudness |
| 8 | Publish | Spreaker API to RSS to Apple, Spotify, Amazon, iHeart; clips to YouTube and Instagram |
| 9 | Learn | Keep winning titles, let losers go dormant. This loop made the flood that platforms read as spam |

## 6. Copy, avoid, do differently

### Copy

| Move | How we do it |
|---|---|
| Orchestration across vendors | Several model tiers behind one pipeline. ElevenLabs primary with a tested fallback voice such as Google Chirp 3 HD (F-TTS-23) |
| Cost per episode and downloads per episode as core metrics | Budget Controller caps from day one |
| Disclosure on every episode | At the end of audio plus metadata (Apple requires it, F-HOST-15). She claims little listener drop-off (F-AUD-05, low-med) |
| Correct-or-remove fast | Written SLA in the corrections policy |
| A B2B line from the same engine | Archive and catalog-to-audio for local publishers. Anchors: $10 per video minute, about $1 per audio episode (F-IPA-09) |
| Cheap utility feeds | Weather, air quality, pollen inside the daily briefs, not as hundreds of feeds |

### Avoid

| Trap | Our rule |
|---|---|
| Feed flooding (325 in a day) | Few feeds per market, slow human-plausible cadence |
| Keyword-chasing on tragedies | Deaths, violence, minors and named private people need a higher bar, a cooling-off delay or human sign-off |
| Invented expert personas, voice clones of real people | Original synthetic voices, labeled |
| Combative PR, volume as the story | Lead with accuracy and show our work |
| Bad legacy content left up | Re-check evergreen and archive episodes too |
| Glitchy audio (groans, repeated words) | Automated audio QA gate before publish |
| Ad-only plan, or planning on a $50 CPM | Sourced CPMs are $12 to $22 (F-ADS-04, 05, 06) |

### The money check *(inference, as of Oct 2026 — verify before buying)*

| | Inception Point | Earlobe local brief (planning case) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per episode | About $1 or less (F-IPA-04) | About $2.56 to $3.16: Claude work $2.00 (F-LLM-07) plus voice $0.56 to $1.16 (F-TTS-17) |
| Downloads per episode | About 69 actual (F-IPA-07) | 40 / 120 / 400 planned (F-AUD-03) |
| Programmatic revenue per episode | About $0.10 to $1.49 | $1.03 at the base case (F-ADS-12) |

| What closes the gap | Nothing disclosed; they pivoted | Direct sponsors: about $4.20 per episode at base if half of 2 slots sell at a $35 CPM (F-ADS-10, unproven); B2B; cheaper voice (F-TTS-12, 23) |

Our verification costs roughly 5x to 20x their LLM spend by design (F-LLM-08). Ads alone lose about $1.5 to $2.1 per episode. Sponsors, B2B and cost discipline decide the business.

### Do differently

| Their way | Our way | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Topic avoidance, no published corrections policy | **Trust Stack** gates every claim: blind source check, fact check, bias check, legal screen, rules-engine decision, public corrections log, monthly transparency report | Answers the exact criticism. Also Product 2 |
| Thousands of keyword shows | Seven wave-0 shows: Columbia Daily, LA Daily, The Breaking Desk, LA Courts Roundup, Case Tracker, Cold Case Files, History of Hollywood. Then NYC and Chicago | Local, courts and archives are the hard-news lane her co-founder said they skipped |
| Programmatic ads | Local direct sponsors first, B2B and syndication second, programmatic fills leftovers | Higher CPMs; pairs with the Trust Stack as a brand-safety story |
| 50 to 120+ personas | 1 to 3 original disclosed hosts per show (mara, theo, lena, marcus, june, ava in `config/shows.yaml`) | Consistency and no impersonation risk |
| No named accountable editor | A named human editor of record *(decision for Joshua and Janel)* | Clearest signal against slop classification |
| Sell AI services to big publishers | **C&C Studio Services**: we set up and distribute real shows for local businesses and people, in person | The human, local version of Liquid Studios (Product 4) |

We complement her rather than compete: she transforms big publishers' existing catalogs; we make original verified local journalism. She may be a referral partner or a reference customer. Outreach angles are in the [career note](notes/jeanine-wright-career.md), section 8.
