About this episode

Taiwan’s present makes more sense once you realize how many different worlds have passed through the island.

This episode traces Taiwan from its Indigenous Austronesian roots through European encounters, Qing rule, Japanese colonialism, the arrival of the Nationalist government after World War II, decades of martial law, and its eventual transformation into a competitive democracy.

We then arrive at the Taiwan of today: a self-governing democracy whose semiconductor industry—especially TSMC—plays an unusually important role in the global technology economy, while Beijing claims sovereignty over the island and cross-strait relations remain a major source of international tension. Rather than predicting what comes next, this episode asks how history, identity, technology, and geopolitics made one island matter far beyond its size.
