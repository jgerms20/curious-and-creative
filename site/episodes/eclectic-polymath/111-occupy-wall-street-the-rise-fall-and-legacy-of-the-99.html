About this episode

Occupy Wall Street didn’t win sweeping legislation. But it permanently changed the language Americans use to talk about inequality.

This episode looks at how the 2008 financial crisis, predatory lending, regulatory failure, and generational frustration created the conditions for Occupy to emerge. It gets into Zuccotti Park, horizontal organizing, leaderless protest, the “99%” slogan, and the movement’s attempt to build a self-governing political community in the middle of New York City.

We also get into why Occupy struggled to convert cultural momentum into policy, how police evictions and internal tensions accelerated its decline, and why its ideas still echo through movements like Fight for $15 and the Debt Collective. This is a story about protest, inequality, and how a movement can lose the immediate fight while still reshaping the political conversation.
