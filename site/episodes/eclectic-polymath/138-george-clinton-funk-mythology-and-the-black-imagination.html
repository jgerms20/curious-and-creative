About this episode

George Clinton didn’t just make funk music. He built an entire universe around it.

This episode traces Clinton’s path from a New Jersey barbershop to the center of Parliament-Funkadelic, where funk, rock, science fiction, street culture, and mythology collided. It gets into the different identities of Parliament and Funkadelic, the musicians who made the collective so powerful, and the creation of the Mothership as one of the most recognizable symbols in Black music.

We also get into synthesizers, sampling, Afrofuturism, intellectual property battles, and the way P-Funk became foundational to hip-hop and generations of experimental Black music. This is a story about sound, spectacle, ownership, and what happens when a band stops being just a band and becomes its own mythology.
