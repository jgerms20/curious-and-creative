About this episode

Mellody Hobson grew up experiencing financial instability. She eventually built a career around understanding exactly how money, ownership, and institutions work.

This episode traces her rise at Ariel Investments from intern to Co-CEO and principal owner, alongside an expanding role in American corporate power—from chairing Starbucks to investing in the Denver Broncos and building Project Black, an effort designed to help minority-owned businesses grow into larger enterprises.

But Hobson’s story is also about a particular philosophy of economic change: financial literacy matters, but ownership changes the equation. We explore her push to be “color brave,” her approach to investing, and how learning the mechanics of capital can become a tool for changing who gets to participate in wealth and power.
