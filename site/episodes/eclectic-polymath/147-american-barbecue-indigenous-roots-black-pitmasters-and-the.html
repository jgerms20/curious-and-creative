About this episode

American barbecue is celebrated as a Southern staple, but its core culinary architecture began with Indigenous fire technology. Long before European contact, Native nations across the Caribbean and the American South had already mastered indirect thermal cooking, engineering raised greenwood frames and earth trenches to slow-smoke meat over days.

This episode traces barbecue back to Taíno and Arawak barbacoa traditions and Southeastern Native smoke practices. It explores how these pre-colonial preservation methods were adopted by early colonizers, adapted through plantation labor, and synthesized with African fire mastery and European livestock.

We get into the physics of earthen pits, the linguistic journey of the word barbacoa, Indigenous woodcraft, and how Native contributions were gradually written out of modern food lore.
