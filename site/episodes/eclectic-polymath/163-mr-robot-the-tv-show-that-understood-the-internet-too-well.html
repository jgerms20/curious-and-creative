About this episode

Mr. Robot felt paranoid when it premiered. A decade later, a lot of what it was worried about feels uncomfortably familiar.

This episode looks at how Sam Esmail turned a hacker drama into one of television’s sharpest portraits of the digital age. We get into the show’s unusually authentic depiction of cybersecurity, its obsession with corporate power and economic inequality, and the real-world anxieties—from the financial crisis to mass surveillance—that shaped Elliot Alderson’s world.

But Mr. Robot was never just about hacking. We also explore Elliot’s trauma, dissociation, unreliable narration, the show’s strange and deliberate cinematography, its alternate reality games, and the deeper question running underneath all four seasons: what happens to human connection when technology, money, institutions, and even our own minds become difficult to trust?
