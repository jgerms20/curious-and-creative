About this episode

The Oslo Accords were supposed to create a path toward peace. Instead, they became the framework through which the conflict kept evolving.

This episode looks at how secret negotiations in Norway produced a phased transition toward Palestinian self-rule while leaving the hardest questions—borders, settlements, Jerusalem, refugees, and final sovereignty—for later. It gets into the First Intifada, the political pressures that brought both sides to the table, the creation of the Palestinian Authority, and the fragile optimism that surrounded the process.

Key points

  • The Oslo Accords were supposed to create a path toward peace.
  • This episode looks at how secret negotiations in Norway produced a phased transition toward Palestinian self-rule while leaving the hardest questions—borders, settlements, Jerusalem, refugees, and final sovereignty—for later.
  • It gets into the First Intifada, the political pressures that brought both sides to the table, the creation of the Palestinian Authority, and the fragile optimism that surrounded the process.
  • We also get into why the framework unraveled: settlement expansion, extremist opposition, the assassination of Yitzhak Rabin, collapsing trust, and the outbreak of the Second Intifada.
  • This is a story about whether Oslo was a sabotaged opportunity for peace or a flawed structure from the beginning—and how that question still shapes the conflict today.
