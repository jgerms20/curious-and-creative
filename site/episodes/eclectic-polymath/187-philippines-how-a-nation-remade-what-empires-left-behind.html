About this episode

The Philippines is a country where Catholic traditions coexist with ancient spiritual beliefs, American influences run through everyday life, and centuries of colonial history have produced a culture unlike anywhere else. But how did thousands of islands become a nation with such a layered identity?

This episode traces the Philippines from precolonial barangays and indigenous mythology through Spanish colonization, the spread of Catholicism, and the revolutionary movement associated with José Rizal. We follow the arrival of American rule, the Philippine-American War, Japanese occupation during World War II, and the eventual overthrow of Ferdinand Marcos through the People Power Revolution.

Key points

  • Along the way, we explore the country's enduring mythology—from Bathala and diwata to the mysterious aswang—alongside its languages, food, religion, and deep connections to American culture.
  • Ultimately, this is a story about cultural adaptation: how a society can absorb influences from across the world without surrendering its own identity, and how history continues to shape what it means to be Filipino.
