About this episode

Brazil’s military dictatorship tried to control not only politics, but culture itself. Musicians faced censorship, surveillance, and repression—and learned to hide criticism inside metaphor, irony, humor, and songs that could mean one thing to a censor and something entirely different to the people listening.

This episode tells that story through Gonzaguinha, the fiercely political singer-songwriter whose life connected some of the biggest forces shaping modern Brazil. From his complicated relationship with his legendary father Luiz Gonzaga and his upbringing in Morro de São Carlos to his emergence during the dictatorship, Gonzaguinha transformed anger about inequality, labor, dignity, and authoritarianism into music that spoke directly to ordinary Brazilians.
