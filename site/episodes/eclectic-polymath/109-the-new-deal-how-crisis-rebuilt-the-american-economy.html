About this episode

The Great Depression didn’t just break the American economy. It forced the country to rethink what government was supposed to do when markets failed.

This episode looks at how Franklin D. Roosevelt’s New Deal transformed the relationship between the federal government, workers, banks, and ordinary citizens. It gets into the structural causes of the Depression, the relief programs that followed, and the creation of institutions like the FDIC and Social Security that permanently changed American economic life.

We also get into the contradictions built into that transformation: reforms that expanded security and opportunity while still excluding many Black Americans, agricultural workers, and other marginalized groups. This is a story about crisis, experimentation, political power, and the moment American capitalism was fundamentally reengineered rather than abandoned.
