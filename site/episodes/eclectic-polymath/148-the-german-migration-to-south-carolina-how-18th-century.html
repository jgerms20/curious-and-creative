#148 The German Migration to South Carolina: How 18th-Century Pioneers Shaped the American South

About this episode

In the 1730s, the British Crown recruited thousands of German-speaking Protestants to the South Carolina backcountry, not just to farm, but to serve as a human buffer between coastal elites, rival empires, and an expanding enslaved majority.

This episode traces the Swiss and German pioneers who settled Orangeburg and the Dutch Fork. It follows how these communities maintained their language and independent churches for generations, integrated into the Southern social order, and saw their public heritage suppressed during World War I.

We get into colonial settlement politics, the Anglicization of regional surnames, frontier church networks, and how this migration wave quietly created South Carolina’s signature mustard-based barbecue.
