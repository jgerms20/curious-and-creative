About this episode

On October 23, 2026, Japan learned that Ryū Murakami was dead, and almost every English-language obituary had to begin the same way: not that Murakami. This episode is about the one the world mostly didn't read: the novelist who grew up in the shadow of an American naval base, won Japan's biggest literary prize at 24 with a novel of drugs and drift, and spent fifty years writing the people postwar Japan's success story left behind.

Key points

  • Coin Locker Babies and the masterpiece that took fifteen years to reach English
  • Audition, Miike, and the Western cult that credited the director for the novelist's machinery
  • In the Miso Soup and the Kabukichō night world
  • and the second act nobody predicted, twenty years hosting Cambria Palace, asking Japan's CEOs the questions.
  • He left that show in April 2026 with a line for the ages: "Writers don't die, they just leave."
