About this episode

The conflict over Taiwan is often framed as a future war waiting to happen. But underneath the military tension is a much older struggle over history, sovereignty, identity, and who gets to define what Taiwan is.

This episode traces the divide from the Chinese Civil War and the 1949 retreat to Taiwan through the island’s democratic transformation and the rise of a distinct Taiwanese identity. It explores Beijing’s push for unification, Taiwan’s resistance to being absorbed, and the United States’ deliberately uncertain policy of strategic ambiguity.

We get into deterrence, military scenarios, semiconductors, economic interdependence, and why a conflict in the Taiwan Strait could send shockwaves through the entire world. This is a story about a small island carrying enormous geopolitical weight—and how peace has been maintained through a balance that grows more fragile every year.
