About this episode

Slavery in Brazil didn’t go unchallenged. Across the country, self-liberated Africans and their descendants built entire communities outside the colonial order.

This episode looks at the quilombos, autonomous societies that ranged from small guerrilla settlements to the massive seventeenth-century Republic of Palmares. It gets into how these communities developed their own political, economic, and military structures, how African traditions mixed with Indigenous knowledge, and how figures like Zumbi and Dandara became symbols of resistance.

We also get into what quilombos became after slavery, from criminalized communities to constitutionally recognized territories, and how Quilombismo turned that history into a broader philosophy of Black liberation. This is a story about resistance, self-government, and the societies people built when freedom had to be created outside the system.
