About this episode

Some ideas are too slippery to understand through explanation alone. Buddhist parables are built for exactly that problem.

This episode looks at how Buddhist stories, koans, and teaching dialogues work as psychological tools designed to interrupt habitual thinking rather than simply deliver a lesson. It gets into skillful means, paradox, metaphor, and the way these stories create moments of confusion that can push a student beyond analysis and toward direct experience.

We also get into examples from the Pali Canon and Zen traditions, and why the most effective teachings are often meant to be discarded once they’ve done their job. This is a story about storytelling as a method of awakening—and how a narrative can sometimes get past the intellect in ways an argument never could.
