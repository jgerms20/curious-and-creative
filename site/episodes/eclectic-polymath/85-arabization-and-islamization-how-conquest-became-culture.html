About this episode

The Arab-Muslim expansion was not just a military story. It was a transformation of language, religion, administration, identity, and culture across societies that had already been shaped by older empires, local traditions, and deep intellectual histories.

This episode looks at how Arabization and Islamization unfolded during the seventh and eighth centuries, as Arab-Muslim rule expanded beyond the Arabian Peninsula into lands once dominated by Byzantine and Sasanian power. Rather than treating conquest as simple cultural erasure, the episode explores how local societies negotiated, adapted, resisted, and reshaped the new imperial order.

Key points

  • The Arab-Muslim expansion was not just a military story.
  • This episode looks at how Arabization and Islamization unfolded during the seventh and eighth centuries, as Arab-Muslim rule expanded beyond the Arabian Peninsula into lands once dominated by Byzantine and Sasanian power.
  • We get into why some regions adopted Arabic more fully while others, like Persia and Armenia, preserved distinct linguistic and cultural identities.
  • This is a story about empire, synthesis, memory, and how conquered peoples helped build the civilization that would come to be called Islamic.
