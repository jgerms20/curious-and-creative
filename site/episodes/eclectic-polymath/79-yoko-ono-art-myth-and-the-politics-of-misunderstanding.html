About this episode

Yoko Ono has spent decades trapped inside one of pop culture’s laziest myths: the woman blamed for breaking up the Beatles. But that story says more about race, misogyny, fandom, and cultural scapegoating than it does about the artist herself.

This episode looks at Ono’s life beyond the caricature, tracing her roots in postwar Japan, her aristocratic upbringing, her survival through trauma, and her emergence as a major figure in conceptual and performance art. Long before she became globally famous through John Lennon, Ono was already pushing the boundaries between artist and audience, object and idea, silence and participation.
