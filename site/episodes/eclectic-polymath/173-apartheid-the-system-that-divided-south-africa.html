About this episode

Apartheid wasn’t simply prejudice written into law. Beginning in 1948, South Africa’s National Party expanded earlier segregation into an elaborate system that classified people by race and controlled where they could live, work, study, travel, and participate politically.

This episode explores how that system actually worked: land restrictions, pass laws, segregated education, labor controls, forced removals, and the creation of designated “homelands.” We also trace the resistance that grew against it, from the ANC and other liberation movements to Sharpeville, Soweto, labor organizing, armed struggle, international pressure, and the imprisonment of figures like Nelson Mandela.

Key points

  • Apartheid wasn’t simply prejudice written into law.
  • Beginning in 1948, South Africa’s National Party expanded earlier segregation into an elaborate system that classified people by race and controlled where they could live, work, study, travel, and participate politically.
  • We follow apartheid through its growing economic and political crisis and the negotiations that finally produced multiracial elections in 1994.
