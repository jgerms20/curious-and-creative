About this episode

Lauren Halsey’s work doesn’t just document South Central Los Angeles. It tries to build something back into it.

This episode looks at how Halsey combines ancient Egyptian imagery, Afrofuturism, funk, neighborhood signage, and monumental architecture to create work that feels both deeply local and globally legible. It traces her rise through the contemporary art world while staying rooted in the people, businesses, and visual language of South Central.

We get into community organizing, the Summaeverythang project, the sister dreamer sculpture park, gentrification, and the tension between making work for elite art institutions and using that visibility to create permanent cultural infrastructure at home. This is a story about art as archive, architecture, and resistance—and what it means to turn cultural capital back toward the community that produced it.
