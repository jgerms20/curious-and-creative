About this episode

Socialism is often treated like one fixed ideology, but its history is really a family of arguments about labor, ownership, inequality, democracy, and what society owes to the people who keep it running.

This episode traces socialism from the upheaval of the Industrial Revolution to Marx and Engels, revolutionary movements, centralized command economies, social democracy, worker cooperatives, and the modern return of socialist language in response to inequality, climate crisis, and capitalism’s failures.

We look at the promise that made socialism so powerful: a society organized around dignity, shared prosperity, and protection from exploitation. But we also look at the price paid under authoritarian regimes, where one-party rule, state control, repression, and economic rigidity turned liberation movements into systems of domination.
