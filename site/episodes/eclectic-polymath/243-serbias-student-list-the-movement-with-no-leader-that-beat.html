About this episode

On October 25, 2026, Serbia handed power to something with no precedent in Europe: a candidate list designed by a leaderless movement — 250 names chosen by student plenums, under rules written to make sure none of them could ever become the story. This episode is how that machine was built, and what it has to survive now that it has won.

Key points

  • The Novi Sad station canopy collapse of November 2024 — sixteen dead, and then an accountability void: indictments filed, charges against six of the accused later dropped with an appeal pending, no trial ever scheduled.
  • open plenums voting on everything, a daily silence at 11:52 in more than 400 towns, the largest protest in Serbia's history, and the Directive
  • no enrolled students, no professional politicians, 250 candidates filtered by plenum veto, one name at a time.
  • Across from it stood fourteen years of media capture, run by a president who had once been Slobodan Milošević's information minister.
  • What Serbia's students studied — Otpor's failure, Italy's Five Star collapse — and what they built differently is the real story.
