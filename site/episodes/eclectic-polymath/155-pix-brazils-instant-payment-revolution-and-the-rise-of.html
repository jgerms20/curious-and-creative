About this episode

Before 2020, Brazilian banking was choked by a private oligopoly that shut millions out of the digital economy with high fees and slow transfer times. Instead of trying to regulate legacy lenders into submission, Brazil’s central bank built its own mandated, zero-cost public rail called Pix—instantly establishing the dominant liquidity engine for South America’s largest economy.

This episode charts how Pix expanded from a simple peer-to-peer tool into a commercial juggernaut that surpassed credit and debit cards while bringing millions of unbanked citizens into the formal system. It also examines the unintended consequences of frictionless money, including how instant liquidity sparked new street crime tactics like express kidnappings.
