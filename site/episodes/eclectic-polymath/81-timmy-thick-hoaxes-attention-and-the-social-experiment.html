About this episode

Social experiments used to belong mostly to psychology labs, classrooms, and controlled research environments. Now they live inside feeds, comment sections, viral personas, platform algorithms, and internet spectacles where audiences often do not realize they are part of the experiment.

This episode looks at the evolution of the social experiment from mid-century academic psychology to the modern attention economy. Through examples like the Stanford Prison Experiment and the viral mystery of Timmy Thick, the story explores how deception, performance, provocation, and public participation became tools for studying and manipulating human behavior.

Key points

  • Social experiments used to belong mostly to psychology labs, classrooms, and controlled research environments.
  • Through examples like the Stanford Prison Experiment and the viral mystery of Timmy Thick, the story explores how deception, performance, provocation, and public participation became tools for studying and manipulating human behavior.
  • We get into the ethical failures behind famous experiments, the rise of institutional hoaxes, the way platforms monetize outrage, and the growing crisis of trust created by AI, algorithms, and corporate manipulation.
