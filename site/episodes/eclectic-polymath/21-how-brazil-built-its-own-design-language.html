About this episode

Welcome back to The Eclectic Polymath Podcast, a curiosity driven deep dive show built around the questions that pull us in.

Brazil’s design story is not just about style, but synthesis, taking outside influences, digesting them, and transforming them into something unmistakably its own.

This episode moves through the idea of anthropophagy, or cultural cannibalism, from the legacy of the 1922 Modern Art Week to Brazilian modernism, architecture, furniture, graphic design, advertising, and the improvisational logic of gambiarra.

Key points

  • We look at how figures like Oscar Niemeyer and Lina Bo Bardi helped shape a visual language that balanced structure with sensuality, modernism with local identity, and global ambition with decolonial imagination.
  • From lyrical curves and brutalist forms to mid century furniture, national branding, and Brazil’s world class creative industries, this is a story about how a country turned cultural mixture into a design philosophy.
