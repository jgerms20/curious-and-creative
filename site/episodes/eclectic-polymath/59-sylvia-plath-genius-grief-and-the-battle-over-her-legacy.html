About this episode

Sylvia Plath is often remembered through the narrow frame of tragedy, but that version of the story can flatten the discipline, ambition, intelligence, and technical mastery that made her one of the most important literary voices of the twentieth century.

This episode looks at Plath beyond the familiar myth of the doomed poet, tracing her early brilliance, creative intensity, psychological pressure, and the constraints of mid-century womanhood. It also explores her volatile marriage to Ted Hughes and the ways her personal life became inseparable from the public interpretation of her work.
