About this episode

Rick Caruso's father swept floors at the Farmers Market. The son built a town next door.

The Grove opened in 2002 after fifteen years of failed schemes and neighborhood war, and then out-sold almost every real mall in America. The Americana opened in Glendale in 2008, backed by a redevelopment agency, fought by the mall across the street all the way to a jury, and walked straight into the financial crisis. Both are still standing. This is the story of the trolley, the fountain, the percentage rents nobody else dared charge, the rival who ended up bankrupt while Caruso bought his anchor store out from under him, and the building philosophy that embarrassed every public megaproject in Los Angeles, including the airport train that still has not opened.
