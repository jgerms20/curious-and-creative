About this episode

Huey Long made government feel real to people who had spent generations watching power work for somebody else.

This episode traces Long’s rise from rural Louisiana to governor, senator, and one of the most disruptive political figures of the Depression era. It gets into the roads, bridges, schools, textbooks, hospitals, taxes, and public services that made poor and working-class Louisianans believe the state was finally delivering for them.

We also get into the machinery behind that success: patronage, intimidation, control of state institutions, attacks on political opponents, mass communication, and Long’s growing national ambition through Share Our Wealth. This is a story about the central contradiction of the Kingfish—how a politician could deliver genuine material progress while steadily dismantling the democratic restraints on his own power.
