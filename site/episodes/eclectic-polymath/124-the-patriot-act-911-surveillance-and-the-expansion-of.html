About this episode

The PATRIOT Act didn’t just change how the United States fought terrorism. It changed the relationship between national security, surveillance, and individual privacy.

This episode looks at how the law emerged in the immediate aftermath of September 11, when fear, urgency, and intelligence failures created enormous pressure to expand federal power. It gets into roving wiretaps, business and library records, Section 215, national security letters, “sneak and peek” searches, information sharing, and the breakdown of barriers between intelligence and law enforcement.

Key points

  • The PATRIOT Act didn’t just change how the United States fought terrorism.
  • This episode looks at how the law emerged in the immediate aftermath of September 11, when fear, urgency, and intelligence failures created enormous pressure to expand federal power.
  • It gets into roving wiretaps, business and library records, Section 215, national security letters, “sneak and peek” searches, information sharing, and the breakdown of barriers between intelligence and law enforcement.
  • We also get into the civil-liberties backlash, the disproportionate impact on Muslim American communities, later legal challenges, the USA FREEDOM Act, and the broader surveillance architecture that outlived many of the law’s most controversial provisions.
