About this episode

FIFA is supposed to govern football, but its power often looks bigger than sport: a private institution with global reach, national influence, billion-dollar commercial machinery, and the ability to shape cities, economies, and political relationships around the world.

This episode looks at FIFA’s evolution from its 1904 founding into one of the most powerful sports organizations on the planet. Through media rights, corporate sponsorships, World Cup hosting, and its one-country, one-vote structure, FIFA became a governing body that blends democratic representation with deep patronage networks.

Key points

  • This episode looks at FIFA’s evolution from its 1904 founding into one of the most powerful sports organizations on the planet.
  • Through media rights, corporate sponsorships, World Cup hosting, and its one-country, one-vote structure, FIFA became a governing body that blends democratic representation with deep patronage networks.
  • We get into the corruption scandals, bribery allegations, RICO indictments, host-nation costs, celebration capitalism, and the modern leadership of Gianni Infantino.
