About this episode

In late August 2016, a mother in Greenville, South Carolina reported what her son had seen in the woods behind their apartment complex: clowns, whispering, flashing green lights, trying to lure children in. Deputies searched the abandoned house by the pond and found nothing. Within nine days the story was national. Within six weeks it was a worldwide panic — school lockdowns, clown hunts, a benched Ronald McDonald, a question at a White House briefing.

Key points

  • In late August 2016, a mother in Greenville, South Carolina reported what her son had seen in the woods behind their apartment complex: clowns, whispering, flashing green lights, trying to lure children in.
  • Within six weeks it was a worldwide panic — school lockdowns, clown hunts, a benched Ronald McDonald, a question at a White House briefing.
  • This episode reconstructs the whole cascade from the Greenville file itself, which is the strangest document in the story: no clown was ever found, photographed credibly, or arrested there, and there was never a case.
  • It also traces the lineage — the identical "phantom clown" wave of 1981, the real costumed clowns of 2013 to 2015 who never caused a panic, and the psychology of why a report with no clown in it reproduced faster than any real one could.
