About this episode

In 1667, England and the Netherlands signed a peace that left the Dutch holding Run, a nutmeg island three kilometres long, and the English holding a muddy port called Manhattan. By the prices of the day, the Dutch won. This episode tells the story of how that could possibly be true: three thousand years of wars fought over flavor.

Key points

  • In 1667, England and the Netherlands signed a peace that left the Dutch holding Run, a nutmeg island three kilometres long, and the English holding a muddy port called Manhattan.
  • By the prices of the day, the Dutch won.
  • China's two-thousand-year state monopoly and the smugglers who nearly toppled dynasties, Sahara caravans trading salt almost weight for weight with gold, France's gabelle and the three thousand people a year punished for salt, and Gandhi's handful of shore salt against an empire.
  • Then pepper, the black gold Rome bled treasure for, that Venice locked down and da Gama broke open with cannon fire.
