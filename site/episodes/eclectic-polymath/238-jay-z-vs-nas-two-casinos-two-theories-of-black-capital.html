About this episode

In September 2025, New York ran the same experiment twice. Jay-Z stood before a community committee and pitched a Caesars casino in the middle of Times Square: "We from these neighborhoods. We are the community." The committee voted him down, 4 to 2. Eight days later, Queens voted 6 to 0 for a $5.5 billion Resorts World expansion with Nas as its hometown face — and the borough president cast his yes while quoting "Queens Get the Money."

This episode reconstructs both fights: the Broadway coalition that killed the Times Square bid, the governor, mayor, and building trades who backed it anyway, and the consent-first Queens campaign that never faced a single speaker against it. It keeps the control cases in frame too: the white-led bids that died by the exact same 4–2 margin, and Steve Cohen's 6–0 win built on years of neighborhood politics first.

Key points

  • Eight days later, Queens voted 6 to 0 for a $5.5 billion Resorts World expansion with Nas as its hometown face — and the borough president cast his yes while quoting "Queens Get the Money."
  • Underneath is the real subject: two theories of Black capital.
  • Jay-Z's seat-at-the-table model — partner with the establishment, convert access into ownership — against Nas's quiet, hometown positioning.
  • One went 2 and 4 with the kingmakers behind him.
  • The other went 6 and 0 — in a house ultimately owned in Kuala Lumpur, on terms nobody has disclosed.
