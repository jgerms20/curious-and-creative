About this episode

Modern wars are usually explained through borders, resources, security, and power. But what happens when governments, movements, and populations begin interpreting conflict through prophecy?

This episode looks at the dangerous overlap between geopolitics and religious belief, tracing the idea of holy war from the Crusades and early Islamic conquests to the present day. It explores how actors connected to the United States, Iran, Israel, and Russia can frame strategic goals through apocalyptic narratives, especially around Jerusalem and competing visions of sacred destiny.

Key points

  • This episode looks at the dangerous overlap between geopolitics and religious belief, tracing the idea of holy war from the Crusades and early Islamic conquests to the present day.
  • It explores how actors connected to the United States, Iran, Israel, and Russia can frame strategic goals through apocalyptic narratives, especially around Jerusalem and competing visions of sacred destiny.
  • We get into the Third Temple, the Mahdi, Christian and Islamic eschatology, the re-sacralization of political conflict, and the risk of self-fulfilling prophecy when people believe war is not only inevitable but divinely ordained.
