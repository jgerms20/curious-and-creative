About this episode

For a hundred years, the polygraph has decided who gets hired, who gets believed, and who goes to prison — without ever actually detecting a lie. This episode traces the machine from John Larson's 1921 Berkeley prototype and the inventor who lived to regret it, through Marston (who also created Wonder Woman's Lasso of Truth), the CIA and NSA screening empires, and the math problem that breaks the whole idea: screen ten thousand people to find ten spies, and you flag a thousand innocents.

Plus the human cost in both directions — Jeffrey Deskovic, who failed a polygraph, falsely confessed, and lost sixteen years; Aldrich Ames, who passed twice while selling secrets — and the twist at the center of it all: the polygraph works, just not as a lie detector. It's a confession machine, and America has never wanted to give it up.
