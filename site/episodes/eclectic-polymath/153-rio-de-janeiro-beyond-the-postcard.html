About this episode

Rio de Janeiro evolved from a disputed colonial outpost and the Americas’ largest slave port into an internationally recognized symbol of tropical modernity. Shaped by dramatic topography and colonial history, the city developed a distinct dual reality defined by stark social divides and extraordinary cultural resilience.

This episode traces how urban displacement and state neglect gave rise to the favelas as vital heartlands of samba, how the beach functions as a shared stage across social classes, and how the city balances its manufactured global image against the everyday lived experience of its communities.
