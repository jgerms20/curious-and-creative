About this episode

Aliko Dangote didn’t build his fortune by chasing the newest industry. He built it by controlling the basic materials economies run on.

This episode traces Dangote’s rise from a Kano merchant family into one of Africa’s most powerful industrialists. We get into cement, sugar, salt, logistics, power generation, and the strategy of replacing imported goods with massive domestic manufacturing systems that could operate despite Nigeria’s infrastructure gaps.

At the center is the Dangote Refinery, an enormous bet on turning Nigeria from a major oil producer that still imports fuel into a country capable of refining far more of its own energy. Dangote’s story becomes a bigger question about African development: can private industrial capital help move economies from exporting raw materials toward actually making more of what they consume?
