Week of February 1, 2026

This Week in AI

The big story this week isn't a new feature or a flashier model—it's where AI actually lives now. OpenAI announced it will start testing ads in ChatGPT. Anthropic's Claude Code went viral with non-developers building real apps. And the data shows Gemini quietly eating into ChatGPT's market share. The theme? AI is becoming infrastructure, not just a tool. Here's what that means for your business.

The Headlines

ChatGPT Is Getting Ads

OpenAI announced Friday it will begin testing advertisements in ChatGPT in the coming weeks. The company needs to offset massive operating costs, and advertising is the obvious revenue lever.

What this means for you: The AI assistant you use every day is about to become an ad platform. If you're a brand, this creates new visibility opportunities—but also new competition for attention. If AI recommendations start mixing paid placements with organic responses, the "trusted advisor" dynamic changes. Worth monitoring.

Claude Code Is Creating a "Viral Moment"

Anthropic's Claude Code is spreading rapidly among non-technical users. People are building functional apps using plain English, and the term "Claude-pilled" is circulating among developers who've switched. The Claude Cowork feature allows complex, multi-step automation on Mac.

What this means for you: This is the moment AI becomes accessible to everyone in your company—not just the tech team. The barrier between "having an idea" and "having a working prototype" just collapsed. If you're not experimenting with these tools internally, your competitors might be.

Google Gemini Is Gaining Fast

ChatGPT's traffic share dropped from 86.7% to 64.5% over the past year. Gemini jumped from 5.7% to 21.5%—nearly a 4x increase. ChatGPT still dominates (800M weekly users vs. Gemini's 600M monthly), but the monopoly is cracking.

What this means for you: Don't build your AI visibility strategy around one platform. Your customers are fragmenting across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude. A multi-platform presence isn't optional anymore—it's necessary.

Deep Dive: The 3x Payoff of Deep AI Integration

This week's AI Daily Brief highlighted a finding that cuts through the hype: companies that deeply integrate AI into core workflows are nearly three times more likely to see real financial gains. Everyone else stalls.

The surveys from PwC, Workday, and Section are being read as "AI is overhyped"—but that misses the point. The capability isn't the bottleneck. Leadership, integration, and execution are.

What separates the winners? They're not just adding AI as a feature. They're rethinking workflows around what AI makes possible. That's the difference between "we have ChatGPT access" and "our proposal process is 60% faster because AI drafts the first version."

If your team is still in the "playing with AI" phase, this is the data point to share with leadership. The ROI isn't theoretical—it's measurable, but only if you commit.

What to Watch

The next few weeks will reveal how ChatGPT handles ads. Will it be subtle contextual placements or disruptive interstitials? Either way, it changes the equation for brands thinking about AI visibility. If you're in ChatGPT's responses today, you might be competing with paid placements tomorrow.

Meanwhile, keep an eye on the "agent swarm" trend. If multiple AI agents can coordinate on complex tasks, the definition of "work" is about to shift again. Early adopters will have a significant head start.
